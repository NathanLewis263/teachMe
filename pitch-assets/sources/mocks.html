How do I find the period of a simple pendulum?

Great question! The period of a simple pendulum is one of the classic results in introductory mechanics, and there are several ways to approach it depending on how rigorous you want to be. Let's walk through the full derivation, the assumptions behind it, and some common extensions.

1. Setting up the problem

Consider a point mass m attached to a massless, inextensible string of length L, pivoting freely about a fixed point. Let θ be the angular displacement from the vertical. The forces acting on the bob are the tension in the string and the gravitational force mg. Resolving gravity into components parallel and perpendicular to the string, the tangential component is −mg sin θ, which acts as the restoring force.

2. Equation of motion

Applying Newton's second law along the arc, with arc length s = Lθ, gives mL d²θ/dt² = −mg sin θ, which simplifies to d²θ/dt² + (g/L) sin θ = 0. This is a nonlinear second-order differential equation, and in general it does not have a solution in terms of elementary functions.
